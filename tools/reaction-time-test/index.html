How to Use This Tool

Step-by-Step Instructions:

  1. Click or tap the test panel to begin a round.
  2. When the panel says Wait for green..., keep your pointer ready but do not click.
  3. Click, tap, or press Space as soon as the panel turns green and displays GO!
  4. Complete five valid rounds to see every result plus your rounded average, best, and worst times.

Tips for a Fair Result:

  • Use the same device and input method when comparing results.
  • Keep your eyes on the center of the panel and react to the color change.
  • Early clicks are discarded and never included in your five valid results or summary.

Understanding Your Reaction Time

Reaction time is the interval between noticing a signal and responding to it. In this test, the signal is a sudden green panel and the response is your click, tap, or key press. The displayed result is measured in milliseconds, where 1,000 milliseconds equals one second.

The wait before each green signal is randomized at runtime. Some rounds begin relatively quickly, others take a medium amount of time, and some take longer. This variation helps prevent you from learning a fixed rhythm and reacting before you actually see the signal.

Results can vary with alertness, practice, input hardware, display refresh rate, browser performance, and whether you use a mouse, touchscreen, or keyboard. For useful comparisons, complete all five valid rounds under similar conditions and focus on the summary rather than a single unusually fast or slow result.

This browser test is designed for casual self-measurement and practice. It is not a medical, neurological, or fitness assessment.

Private Browser Processing

The timer and results run entirely in your browser. Your reaction-time attempts are not uploaded or saved by this tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good reaction time?

Visual reaction times vary by person, device, and testing conditions. Many adults record results around 200 to 300 milliseconds in a simple browser test, but hardware and input lag can influence the number.

Why is the waiting time different every round?

An unpredictable wait makes it difficult to anticipate the green signal. Each attempt receives a newly generated delay, including retries after an early click.

Does a “Too soon!” attempt count?

No. Clicking before green immediately cancels that round, and the attempt is excluded from both your result and valid attempt count.
